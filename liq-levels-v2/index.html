How to read it. Fresh = liquidation leverage Coinglass still shows (positions opened in the last 48h). Aged = the 6-hour slices that rolled off that window since, kept on the fixed grid, removed where price has traded through them, faded on a 72h half-life, dropped after 7 days. Roll-off residue = the part of each slice difference that is negative (liquidity the newer fetch shows that the older one did not), after both maps are smoothed; it is the noise floor of the memory — under ~10% the subtraction is clean, above it that slice is suspect.

HEATMAP

COINGLASS MODEL 3 · 48H HEATMAP · 2026-10-13

Sources & method. Updated 2026-10-13 12:45 PT. Every hour the Coinglass Model 3 48h heatmap payload is captured and rebinned onto a fixed $50 grid. Every six hours the positions that rolled off Coinglass's 48h window since the last slice (that capture's latest column minus the current column at the same timestamp, both Gaussian-smoothed 3 bins) are stored as an aged slice, removed wherever price has since traded through it, faded on a 72h half-life, and dropped after 7 days. Levels are bands on the combined fresh + aged map: a Gaussian-smoothed profile (σ = 0.10% of spot), local peaks, edges at 70% of the peak, up to four per side excluding ±0.25% around spot; a level from the previous run survives while a band still overlaps it, it has not been swept, and its mass is at least 60% of the weakest newcomer. Each run logs its levels and a mirror control per level (same distance, other side, no band there) and scores them 48h later: touched or not, and the OKX BTC-USDT perpetual liquidation notional printed inside the band around the touch.
