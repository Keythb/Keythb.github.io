About the Name

TON 618

The fund is named for a black hole — the reasons are below, along with what the name does not mean.

The Object

An ultramassive black hole

TON 618 was catalogued in 1957, in a survey of faint blue objects photographed at the Tonantzintla Observatory in Puebla, Mexico — the “TON” is Tonantzintla, and 618 is simply its number in the catalogue. It looked like an unremarkable star. It was later identified as a quasar: the luminous core of a distant galaxy, shining because matter is falling into the black hole at its center.

The black hole powering TON 618 is one of the most massive ever measured — estimates run to tens of billions of solar masses, a class astronomers call ultramassive. Its event horizon would swallow the entire solar system many times over. The quasar it powers is among the most luminous objects known, radiating with the brightness of trillions of suns. The light in our sky tonight left it more than ten billion years ago.

Why We Chose It

Gravity, and the light it throws off

TON618 Capital is a concentrated fund built around one gravitational thesis — Bitcoin — with a research bench in the technology companies being pulled into the same orbit. The name is the investment philosophy: one idea so powerful that it consumes everything caught in its gravity well.

A black hole is invisible; you know it by what it does. A quasar is that same invisible mass made into the brightest object in the sky. That is the second half of the name: the position is the mass, and the published research is the light — every thesis we act on is written up, put on the record, and scored in public on the track record.
