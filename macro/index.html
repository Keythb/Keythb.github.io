Regime
Fiscal dominance, hawkish hold, cushioned consumer
The sovereign is fiscally dominant: a third of ~$39T reprices each year, so the average coupon ratchets toward market rates and a hawkish Fed raises interest expense faster than it cools a supply-driven inflation. The Fed is holding, not cutting, and the 10-year is doing the tightening. The consumer has not felt it because three stores are being drawn down first — household savings, corporate margin, and point-of-sale credit that the revolving statistics do not record. The most probable multi-year resolution is financial repression plus stealth monetization. The view is timed by the exhaustion of the stores, not by a single print.
Counter-case, carried: Core three-month momentum decelerated four straight months into June and the 5-year breakeven has been falling since spring. If the anchor holds and core PCE returns to 2% annualized without cuts, the repression base case fails (trigger de-core-pce-back-to-2) and the whole regime label is wrong.

THE EQUATION

Δd  =  ((r − g) / (1 + g)) × dt−1  −  pb
g  =  π + productivity + hours
d = debt held by the public / GDP · r = average rate on marketable debt · g = nominal GDP growth · pb = primary balance / GDP (surplus positive). The ratio rises when the rate on the debt exceeds nominal growth and the primary balance does not cover the gap. Productivity is the only lever in g that raises real output without feeding back into r.

mv-three-cushionsLIVE
Three stores suppress the two variables the Fed is mandated to watch. Corporate margin absorbs cost so the price index never records it; household savings absorb price rises so demand never weakens (of $1,185bn of consumption growth to April, ~50% came from the saving rate falling 5.5% to 3.0% against 9% from credit); and point-of-sale payment-spreading builds leverage the revolving statistics do not record. None appears in employment or prices until exhausted.

The debt equation and the consumer cushions are the same clock read from two sides: r ratchets up as the coupon store rolls, g holds up as the household stores drain.
