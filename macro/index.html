Regime
Fiscal dominance, hiking Fed, cushioned consumer
The sovereign is fiscally dominant: a third of ~$39T reprices each year, so the average coupon ratchets toward market rates and a hawkish Fed raises interest expense faster than it cools a supply-driven inflation. The Fed hiked 25bp to 3.75-4.00% in September, 12-0, and its dots show no cuts through 2028; the 10-year rose 28bp into the decision rather than falling on it. The consumer has not felt it because three stores are being drawn down first — household savings, corporate margin, and point-of-sale credit that the revolving statistics do not record. The most probable multi-year resolution is financial repression plus stealth monetization. The view is timed by the exhaustion of the stores, not by a single print.
Counter-case, carried: Core three-month momentum has decelerated five straight months (4.76% in February to 3.05% on the July vintage), and the Fed hiked through it — the September decision set a level-and-duration standard, not a momentum one. If the anchor holds and core PCE returns to 2% annualized without cuts, the repression base case fails (trigger de-core-pce-back-to-2) and the whole regime label is wrong.

THE EQUATION

Δd  =  ((r − g) / (1 + g)) × dt−1  −  pb
g  =  π + productivity + hours
d = debt held by the public / GDP · r = average rate on marketable debt · g = nominal GDP growth · pb = primary balance / GDP (surplus positive). The ratio rises when the rate on the debt exceeds nominal growth and the primary balance does not cover the gap. Productivity is the only lever in g that raises real output without feeding back into r.
