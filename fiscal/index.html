EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

IS THIS WHAT THE START OF A SOVEREIGN DEBT CRISIS LOOKS LIKE?
No — nothing here looks like the start of a sovereign debt crisis today.
But the system is running with less shock-absorbing capacity than usual: composite 51 (WATCH), 2 of 7 plumbing stress flags up. That is strain, not crisis — the difference is that every channel below is still functioning. Strain matters because it decides how well the system would take a shock, not because it causes one.
A sovereign funding crisis can only arrive through one of four doors — buyers refusing new auctions, existing holders selling the stock, the funding market seizing, or the interest arithmetic compounding. Where each stands today:
AUCTION DEMANDSOLID — A debt crisis starts here, as a buyers' strike: auctions tailing, dealers forced to absorb what investors refuse. Today buyers are showing up — foreign (indirect) bidders took 75.4 % of recent long-end auctions, bid-to-cover is 2.51 x, and dealers were left with only 8.3 %. Nothing in the auction tape resembles a buyers' strike.
FOREIGN HOLDERSSELLING — A crisis can also arrive from the stock rather than the flow: existing holders selling, which no auction statistic can see. NY Fed custody for foreign officials — where a central bank selling Treasuries to defend its currency shows up within days — moved -30 $B over four weeks; Japan, the largest foreign holder and the live intervention case, moved -96 $B over three months on the lagged TIC data (part of that is yields marking the stock down, not selling). Custody falling at this pace is what an official-sector selldown looks like while it is happening — this door is open, watch it weekly.
FUNDING PLUMBINGTIGHTENING — The third route is a funding-market seizure — cash refusing to flow against good collateral, the 2019-repo shape. 2 of 7 stress flags are up (Curve noise, SOFR scarcity), and the overnight buffer that used to absorb these shocks is effectively spent (ON RRP at 1 $B), so strain now transmits instead of being absorbed. One more flag would enter the backtested trigger zone. SOFR scarcity is the one flag here with stand-alone predictive value for funding blowups, and it is up.
DEBT ARITHMETICCORROSIVE — The last route is not an event but arithmetic: debt carrying an average coupon of 3.48% reprices toward a 10-year at 4.77% — $10.8T of it within twelve months, and interest already absorbs 20.1 % of federal receipts. The one genuinely favorable structural reading: nominal growth still outruns the average coupon (r−g at -3.09 pp), which keeps this corrosive rather than explosive. It cannot cause a crisis this month; it makes every future shock harder to absorb, and it does not care what the rest of this page says.
CHANGED THIS MONTHThe composite slipped 1.3 points over the past month (from 52 on 2026-08-07). Indirect (foreign) award share improved most (70 → 95) and Foreign official custody 4-week change deteriorated most (73 → 36). Flag count unchanged from a month ago.
WHAT TO WATCH NEXT
1. Auction demand cracking. The indirect share and bid-to-cover at the next long-end auctions (now 75.4 % and 2.51 x) — a sharp drop in both with dealers forced to take more is the classic first domino.
2. Foreign official custody falling week after week (4-week change now -30 $B). Sustained declines with the lagged TIC Japan line confirming is the signature of intervention-funded Treasury selling — the earliest visible form of the foreign-holder door opening.
3. SOFR staying pinned above IORB (now +1.0 bps, flag up). The one flag with stand-alone predictive value for funding blowups; it clearing would be the single best news on this page.
4. A third stress flag. At 2 of 7, one more flag enters the backtested trigger zone.
5. The composite crossing into STRESS (below 45; now 5.8 points above the line). That would move the verdict at the top of this box.
