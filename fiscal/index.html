EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

IS THIS WHAT THE START OF A SOVEREIGN DEBT CRISIS LOOKS LIKE?
Warning signs are present.
The composite is at 49 (WATCH) with 4 of 7 plumbing stress flags up. This is the configuration in which funding stress events have historically clustered — not a declaration of crisis, but the channels below deserve daily attention.
A sovereign funding crisis can only arrive through one of four doors — buyers refusing new auctions, existing holders selling the stock, the funding market seizing, or the interest arithmetic compounding. Where each stands today:
AUCTION DEMANDSOLID — A debt crisis starts here, as a buyers' strike: auctions tailing, dealers forced to absorb what investors refuse. Today buyers are showing up — foreign (indirect) bidders took 74.3 % of recent long-end auctions, bid-to-cover is 2.55 x, and dealers were left with only 6.8 %. Nothing in the auction tape resembles a buyers' strike.
FOREIGN HOLDERSSELLING — A crisis can also arrive from the stock rather than the flow: existing holders selling, which no auction statistic can see. NY Fed custody for foreign officials — where a central bank selling Treasuries to defend its currency shows up within days — moved -19 $B over four weeks; Japan, the largest foreign holder and the live intervention case, moved -106 $B over three months on the lagged TIC data (part of that is yields marking the stock down, not selling). Custody falling at this pace is what an official-sector selldown looks like while it is happening — this door is open, watch it weekly.
FUNDING PLUMBINGSTRESSED — The third route is a funding-market seizure — cash refusing to flow against good collateral, the 2019-repo shape. 4 of 7 stress flags are up (UST fails, Curve noise, Discount window, SOFR scarcity), and the overnight buffer that used to absorb these shocks is effectively spent (ON RRP at 0 $B), so strain now transmits instead of being absorbed. Three or more flags is the zone that historically carried roughly twice the normal hazard of a market stress event over the following quarter — elevated odds of turbulence, not a prediction, and most such episodes resolve without one. SOFR scarcity is the one flag here with stand-alone predictive value for funding blowups, and it is up.
DEBT ARITHMETICCORROSIVE — The last route is not an event but arithmetic: debt carrying an average coupon of 3.52% reprices toward a 10-year at 5.31% — $10.6T of it within twelve months, and interest already absorbs 20.3 % of federal receipts. The one genuinely favorable structural reading: nominal growth still outruns the average coupon (r−g at -3.30 pp), which keeps this corrosive rather than explosive. It cannot cause a crisis this month; it makes every future shock harder to absorb, and it does not care what the rest of this page says.
CHANGED THIS MONTHThe composite slipped 1.5 points over the past month (from 50 on 2026-09-04). Days SOFR ≥ IORB (last 20) improved most (45 → 85) and UST dealer fails-to-deliver (4w avg) deteriorated most (57 → 19). Flags up from 2 a month ago.
WHAT TO WATCH NEXT
1. Auction demand cracking. The indirect share and bid-to-cover at the next long-end auctions (now 74.3 % and 2.55 x) — a sharp drop in both with dealers forced to take more is the classic first domino.
2. Foreign official custody falling week after week (4-week change now -19 $B). Sustained declines with the lagged TIC Japan line confirming is the signature of intervention-funded Treasury selling — the earliest visible form of the foreign-holder door opening.
3. SOFR staying pinned above IORB (now -1.6 bps, flag up). The one flag with stand-alone predictive value for funding blowups; it clearing would be the single best news on this page.
4. The flag count. At 4 of 7 the joint-stress alarm is in its tested zone; falling back to one or zero clears it, a fourth flag deepens it.
5. The composite crossing into STRESS (below 45; now 3.8 points above the line). That would move the verdict at the top of this box.

THE READAI-written interpretation

The page's strain is not a buyers' strike. Auction demand scores 79 and is the healthiest reading here, which means nothing on this page shows investors declining to fund the Treasury at a price. The composite at 49 is registering cost and collateral, not appetite. The four of seven plumbing alarm sits almost entirely in settlement and curve-fitting measures, while the one flag the backtest credits with stand-alone value is a rate still sitting below IORB.

The sharper contradiction is arithmetical. r minus g scores 100, the best reading on the page, while the fuse gap scores 10 — on the same debt. Growth still outruns the average coupon, and the average coupon sits 1.79 pp below what new debt costs. The favorable term is the one that decays mechanically. Read the strain accordingly: corrosive rather than acute, with the backtest's own missed unwind arguing against treating the flag count as a clock.
